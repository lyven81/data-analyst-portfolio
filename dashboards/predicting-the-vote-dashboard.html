The forecast, before the vote

The projection: seats by coalition

Built from the 2018 and 2022 results and the 2020 census, with nothing from the 2026 vote. The method projects BN 48, PH 6, PN 1, and one seat to a small party, the 2022 picture with the recent swing carried one step further.

The 14 seats the forecast flags as contests

The method is sure of 42 seats and flags 14 as genuine contests. These are the seats to watch: mostly mixed seats (30 to 50 percent Chinese) where the swing lands the result near even. Each bar shows how sure the method is of its call in that seat.

Forecast versus reality

Forecast versus the actual result, by coalition

The forecast called 53 of 56 seats and matched BN's total exactly. On the 14 genuine contests it went 11 of 14, about 8 in 10. Every seat it got wrong was one it had already flagged.

Forecast share versus actual share, one dot per seat

Each dot is a seat: forecast BN share across, actual up. The line is a perfect forecast. Almost every dot sits above it, so BN did better than the forecast nearly everywhere. The method names the winner well but under-calls the margin, by 13.5 points on average.

What decides a seat

Which side a seat locks for, by its community make-up

A seat's community make-up settles which side it is safe for. Malay-majority seats lock for BN, Chinese-majority seats for PH, and only the mixed middle is genuinely in play. This is not a story of one community deciding the election; it is about which side each kind of seat is already safe for. Note the ends: Chinese-majority seats are only 5 of 56 and all lock for PH, while the many Malay-majority seats form BN's floor.

Where the 2026 surge came from

The landslide was built on a swing, and the swing was largest in Malay-majority seats, where BN beat the forecast by about 20 points, and smallest in Chinese-majority ones. The movement that turned a majority into a landslide came from Malay-majority seats, not Chinese. Chinese-majority seats stayed locked for PH and did not shape the outcome.

So who shapes the result?

Two things, and neither is the Chinese-majority seat. Who governs was decided by BN's floor of Malay-majority safe seats, which alone passed the majority. The size of the win was decided by the mixed seats and the Malay swing. The Chinese-majority seats are a small, locked minority. In a more balanced state the mixed seats would decide government too, which is why they matter most for Negeri Sembilan.

Four hypotheses to test in Negeri Sembilan

1. Malay-majority seats favour BN. BN won all 29 Johor seats under 30 percent Chinese, and every seat over 65 percent Malay.
2. Chinese-majority seats favour PH. BN won none of the five seats over 50 percent Chinese.
3. The locks are about 50 percent Chinese and 65 percent Malay. A 75 percent super-majority adds no further certainty.
4. Mixed seats (30 to 50 percent Chinese) are the battleground. The only band in doubt (86 percent BN), holding 10 of 14 contests and most of the seats that changed hands.
