The race to a majority

Each bloc's safe floor, plus the seats it takes from the six

A majority is 19 of 36. Neither bloc gets there on its safe seats alone: the BN-PN floor is 17 and the PH floor is 13. The six break PH five, BN-PN one, so each side lands on 18. Neither crosses the line, and the assembly is hung.

The range: three ways the six can break

The outcome depends on how much of the pact vote Bersatu takes in the close seats. If the pact holds Labu, it keeps a 19-17 majority. If Labu flips, as its sitting-member candidacy suggests, the assembly is level at 18-18: the central case. If Paroi flips too, PH edges a 19-17 majority. Across the plausible range the result runs from a bare BN-PN majority to a bare PH one, centred on a hung assembly.

The six, who leads each contest

The six, three ways: points for each side, and who leads

For each of the six, PH's 2023 share against the pact candidate and Bersatu once the non-PH vote divides. The tallest bar wins the seat. PH leads five (Ampangan, Klawang, Pilah, Sikamat, Labu); the pact holds Paroi. This is an illustrative central split: the exact Bersatu share is the unknown, so read it as the central scenario, not a fixed prediction.

Settled seats versus seats in play

Sorted by certainty, the board is 30 settled and 6 in play. The settled 30 split BN-PN 17, PH 13, so they cancel close to even and hand the decision to the six. Inside the six, five carry a third candidate, and they break PH five, BN-PN one.

What the split does

The six, head to head: PH against the pooled pact

Each seat shows PH's 2023 share against the pooled BN-PN share. PH leads four outright (Ampangan, Klawang, Pilah, Sikamat). In Labu and Paroi the pact leads on the two-way score, but a third candidate, Bersatu, now splits that pact bar. A split of about 15% of the pact vote flips Labu to PH, where the Bersatu candidate is the sitting member; Paroi needs about 24% and is more likely to hold.
■ PH share    ■ Pooled BN-PN share (before the split)

Bersatu: spoiler or kingmaker? It turns on Labu

Bersatu is placed to win at most one seat, Labu. If Labu goes to PH, Bersatu holds nothing and is a pure spoiler: the split alone levels the assembly at 18 each. If the sitting member holds Labu, the count is PH 17, BN-PN 18, Bersatu 1, and in a hung chamber that single seat is the one that lets the pact reach 19. Its weight is large as a splitter and small as a seat-winner.
■ PH    ■ BN-PN    ■ Bersatu    Both scenarios are hung; only the second makes Bersatu a kingmaker.

What decides a seat, and every seat

Which bloc a seat locks for, by its community make-up

A seat's make-up sorts the map. PH wins almost every non-Malay and mixed seat; BN-PN wins almost every Malay-majority seat. This is a two-sided lock, not one community deciding the state: each kind of seat is already safe for a different side. A third Malay candidate reopens the Malay-majority band that is not quite lopsided enough to settle, which is where the six sit.
