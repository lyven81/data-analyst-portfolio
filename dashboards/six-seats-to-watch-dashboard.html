The race to a majority

Each bloc's safe floor, plus the seats it takes from the six

A majority is 19 of 36. Neither bloc gets there on its safe seats alone: the BN-PN floor is 17 and the PH floor is 13. The central forecast tops each up from the six contests, BN-PN by two and PH by four, landing on 19 to 17. Every seat above the line comes from the six.

Distance to a majority: how much of the six each bloc still needs

The maths is lopsided. From 17 safe seats the BN-PN bloc needs only two of the six to reach 19, and it is favoured in two of them. From 13 safe seats PH would have to win all six, including the two that lean BN-PN, just to reach a bare majority. That asymmetry, not a statewide mood, is why one bloc is the clear favourite.
Bars show seats still needed from the six-seat battleground to reach 19. Six are available.

How settled is it, really?

Winning margins across all 36 seats

The one-seat headline hides how lopsided the map is. 25 of the 36 seats are won by more than 40 points, and only six are decided by less than 15. The election looks close in the seat total, but nearly every individual seat is a blowout. The contest lives entirely in the thin left tail.

Settled seats versus seats in play

Sorted by certainty, the board is 30 settled and 6 in play. The settled 30 split BN-PN 17, PH 13, so they cancel close to even and hand the decision to the six. The six are the whole election; the other 30 only set the starting line.

How sure is the method?

How settled each seat is, by the winning margin

The method is sure of the 30 settled seats, each won by more than 15 points, and flags exactly six as contests. The split is clean: no settled seat is closer than 23 points, and every one of the six is under 13, so a roughly 10-point gap separates the watch-list from the safe seats. Nothing close hides outside the six, which is what lets the forecast name a short watch-list instead of claiming false certainty across the board.
Seats grouped by how far the winning bloc leads. The 30 settled seats are sure; the six are the watch-list.

The six contests, head to head

Each bar pair is one contest: PH's share against the combined BN-PN share. Ampangan is neck-and-neck, while Paroi has the clearest lead of the six. The four the method leans PH (Ampangan, Klawang, Pilah, Sikamat) and the two it leans BN-PN (Labu, Paroi) are exactly what delivers the 19-17 result.

What decides a seat, and every seat

Which bloc a seat locks for, by its community make-up

A seat's make-up sorts the map. PH wins almost every non-Malay and mixed seat; BN-PN wins almost every Malay-majority seat. This is a two-sided lock, not one community deciding the state: each kind of seat is already safe for a different side. The contest survives only in the Malay-majority band that is not quite lopsided enough to settle, which is where the six sit.
